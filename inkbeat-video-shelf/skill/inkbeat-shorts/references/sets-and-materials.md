# Sets and materials

## Painted surfaces, never flat color
Every set material has three layers:
- a large value drift (±4–8% over 0.3–1 m);
- brush strokes along the grain (±3%);
- edge wear (a 1–2 px light top edge, grime in the corners).

Each board, tile or brick varies ±5% in value and ±3° in hue. No per-pixel noise below 3 px. Aim for a **texture
energy ≥ 0.45** (a flat-color first pass measured ~0.19). Ink density and texture energy are the two numbers that most
separate a premium frame from a cheap one.

### Per-material recipes
- planks: boards 0.15–0.25 m, seams, staggered joints, ±6% per board;
- siding: 0.15–0.2 m boards, a dark seam plus a 1 px light bevel;
- roof tiles and shingles: staggered rows 0.2–0.25 m with a row-shadow line, ±8%, a few darker or mossy;
- stone 0.4 × 0.25 m and brick 0.22 × 0.07 m, mortar 1.5–2 px, chipped corners, moss at the base;
- plaster: a ±3% mottle, grime toward the floor, a wallpaper motif every 60–80 px;
- metal: toon bands plus one sharp streak;
- fabric: soft two-tone folds and stitched edges.

Texture lines (planks, shingles, joints) are painted into the textures at 1–2 px, not inked in post.

### Textures must not read as dirt
- Flat painted surfaces stay quiet (detail strength): plaster 0.25, wood 0.22, metal 0.15, paper 0.3, fabric 0.45.
- Structural materials stay strong: planks and stone 0.6, brick, roof tiles and shingles 0.7, rock 0.6, grass 0.55.
- Object-space paint drift ~0.025; clarity sharpen ~0.22 (0.45 pumped the brush scribbles into grime).

### Gloss
A light bevel highlight line on the top edges of boxy props gives a toy sheen. Keep everything else matte-ish.

## Dense set dressing
- **≥ 12 distinct props in view in every framing**, plus ground clutter on every walkable surface (references show
  12–25 props and hundreds of small repeated elements per frame; a sparse first pass had 3–8).
- A foreground element within 1–2 m of the lens (DOF-blurred) in about half the shots.
- One story prop for the line being spoken.
- No empty or unfinished set areas and no meaningless backdrops.

What to dress, for example:
- houses: 2–3 lit windows with curtains and interior silhouettes, a porch lamp, shutters, a chimney, a gutter and
  downspout, a flower box, a house number; vary roof color and pitch;
- a market street: stalls with awnings, crates and barrels, a lantern, signs, a bench, a bulletin board;
- every window shows something behind it (an interior or the outside view).

## Real scale and toy feel
With a character ≈ 1.6–2.0 m tall:
- door 2.05–2.3 m, window 1.1–1.3 m, table 0.75 m, counter 0.9 m;
- lamp post 3.5–4 m, barrel 1.0 m, crate 0.6–0.8 m;
- house eave 3.2–3.8 m, two-story house 6–7.5 m;
- kids 0.85–1.45 m.

Props are toy-like: slightly chunky, rounded bevels, thick boards.

## Sky, clouds, sea, weather
- The sky is a clean three-stop gradient with no noise.
- Clouds: layered painterly lobes, the lit side +15–25 L\*, soft lit rims (never thin white outlines), parallax at 2–3
  depths. Never flat 2D cut-outs.
- Water and splashes as clean cartoon shapes with outlines.
- Rain only in exterior worlds and outside windows, never inside a room; vary its length, opacity and depth.

## No real brands
No real brands or logos on props and screens: phones and laptops without real brand marks, invented product labels.
